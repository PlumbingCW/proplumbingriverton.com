Sewer Camera Inspection in Riverton, UT

A sewer camera inspection in Riverton, UT sends a waterproof camera through the pipe between your house and the public main, so you can see roots, cracks, sags and blockages on video instead of guessing. Licensed and insured plumbers run the camera and walk you through what it shows.

People book it for slow drains with no clear cause, before buying a home, after a repair, or before spending money on digging. You get a clear price before work starts.

The findings

What the camera video actually shows

A pipe that looks fine from the outside can be failing inside. The camera head has lights and a sealed lens, and it records as it is pushed down the line, so every joint and bend is on video.

How it goes

How an inspection visit works

1

Find the access

Usually a cleanout. If there isn't one, we may pull a toilet or work from a roof vent, and we'll say so first.

2

Clear what blocks the view

A full pipe can't be filmed. We may need a light cable pass or jetting before the lens can see.

3

Push the camera downstream

We note the distance to each defect as the footage records.

4

Locate and mark

A locator finds the camera's transmitter from above, so we can paint a mark on the ground and read the depth.

5

Review it with you

We show the screen, point out each finding and tell you what we'd do about it, including doing nothing.

Buying a home

A camera check before you buy

Standard home inspections often do not send a camera down the sewer lateral. If you buy the house, you own the private line from the home to the main, along with whatever is wrong with it.

Ask the seller for access to the cleanout and schedule the camera before your inspection period ends. If it finds roots or a broken section, you can negotiate a repair or a credit before closing.

  • A clear video won't prove the line stays clear. It shows its condition on that day.
  • Roots in an old clay lateral are common, and a root-cleaned pipe can still be sound.
  • A broken or collapsed section is the finding that changes the price you offer.

Locating

Finding where the pipe runs and where it fails

Locating is a separate skill from filming. A transmitter in the camera head sends a signal, and a handheld receiver above ground picks it up. That gives the line's path and the depth to the defect.

This matters before any dig. We don't guess where a buried problem is, we locate it, then call 811 through Blue Stakes of Utah so other utilities are marked before anyone breaks ground.

Ownership

What a Riverton inspection covers and what it doesn't

Jordan Basin Improvement District (JBID) maintains the sewer main, and you own the private line to it. Our camera covers your line, and if the footage suggests a main problem, we'll tell you and point you to the district at (801) 571-1166.

JBID notes that clay pipes from before the 1980s are prone to roots. We also inspect lines for homeowners in South Jordan and Bluffdale, where the same district serves the mains.
