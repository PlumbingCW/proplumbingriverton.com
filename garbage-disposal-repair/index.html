Triage

Jam, motor or leak: sort it in three questions

What each symptom usually points to
SymptomLikely problemRepair or replace
Loud hum, blades do not turnJammed flywheelUsually repair: free the jam, check for damage
Silent, no hum, reset button poppedOverheated motor or tripped overloadReset once. If it trips again, replace
Dead silent, reset does nothingDead outlet, switch or burned-out motorTest power first, then likely replace
Water at the top ringFailed sink flange sealReseal or reinstall, often repair
Water at the side hoseLoose dishwasher or drain connectionTighten or replace the hose, repair
Water from the housing bottomCracked housing or failed internal sealReplace the unit

That third leak is the clearest sign of an end-of-life disposal. A unit that drips from the lower housing cannot be sealed from outside, so repair costs more than it saves.

While you wait

Safe steps to take right now

Safe to do yourself

  • Switch the disposal off at the wall and unplug it if there is a plug
  • Shine a flashlight in and lift out anything you can see with long tongs
  • Press the red reset button on the bottom after it has cooled
  • Insert a hex key into the bottom socket and work it back and forth to free a jam

Leave it to a plumber

  • Reaching a hand or fingers into the chamber, ever
  • Running it again after water appears under the cabinet
  • Any wiring beyond the plug, switch or cord
  • Removing the unit when the sink drain line behind it is also blocked

If the hex key moves freely and the reset holds, run cold water and test briefly. If it hums again with no movement, stop. Forcing a jammed motor burns it out.

Keep it clear

What never belongs in a disposal

A disposal grinds food, but it does not move it far. Everything it creates still has to travel down the kitchen drain, and that is where trouble builds. Jordan Basin Improvement District says not to pour cooking grease down any drain, even with a disposal. Put it in the trash.

Grease cools in the pipe, coats the walls and catches scraps until the line narrows. That is also why a sink can back up with a perfectly healthy disposal.

  • Cooking oil, fat and bacon grease
  • Coffee grounds in quantity, which pack like sand
  • Rice, pasta and bread, which swell with water
  • Fibrous food such as celery, corn husks and potato peels
  • Bones, fruit pits and anything hard
  • Glass, metal, plastic, rubber bands or cleaning rags
Safety first

What to do before anyone touches the disposal

  • ✓Cut the power at the switch and the breaker.A disposal that looks dead can still start. Never rely on the wall switch alone.
  • ✓Keep hands out of the opening.Do not reach into the chamber, even with the power off. Use tongs or pliers for anything you can see.
  • ✓Close the sink drain.If water is leaking, put a bucket under the unit and keep the faucet off until the leak is found.
  • ✓Skip the chemicals.Do not pour drain cleaner into a jammed disposal. It splashes back and can burn skin and eyes.

When a disposal should be replaced

Repair makes sense for a jam, a loose hose or a loose flange. Replacement makes sense when the motor is dead, the housing leaks or the unit has jammed repeatedly with the same grinding noise. A cheaper unit does not always mean lower cost if it needs another call a year later.

When we replace one, we match the mounting ring to your sink, hook up the drain and dishwasher line, and test it with water running. If your dishwasher connection leaks at the disposal, we fix it at the same visit.

Planning to install a new disposal

Installs go faster when there is an outlet under the sink and the cabinet has room for the unit. If either is missing, tell us on the call. A disposal tied into a drain that is already slow should have that line checked first.

Riverton drains

Kitchen sink clogs and the sewer line behind them

If water backs up into the sink when the disposal runs, the blockage may sit past the disposal, in the kitchen drain line. A cable machine clears most of these. See drain cleaning for how that works.

Jordan Basin Improvement District offers a simple check. If you find standing water at your cleanout, the blockage is likely between the house and the sewer main. If you find none, the problem is likely inside the house.

The district maintains the main. The private line from home to main belongs to the homeowner.

Do not run the disposal while the sink is full of backed-up water. You will only push waste into a line that cannot take it.

Rules and sources

Rules and sources behind this page

Disposals connect to both the drain and the home's electrical circuit, so the plumbing side follows the IPC. Utah Code 15A-2-103 adopts the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as the state plumbing code, effective July 1, 2026, with Utah amendments on top. Wiring is a separate trade, and a plumber will tell you when an electrician needs to look at the circuit.

According to Jordan Basin Improvement District guidance, cooking grease should go in the trash instead of down the drain, even with a disposal running. That guidance applies to every Riverton kitchen.
