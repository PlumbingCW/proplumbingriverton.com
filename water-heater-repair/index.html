When a leaking water heater is an emergency

A slow drip is a repair problem. A heater pouring water, or sitting on finished flooring, is an emergency, because a full tank holds a lot of water and a failing seam can empty it fast. Do these things in order while you wait.

1

Smell for gas first

A rotten-egg smell means leave the building and call Enbridge Gas Utah at 1-800-767-1689 or 911 from outside. Do not flip switches. Call us after the gas utility says it is safe.

2

Shut the cold supply

Turn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise until it stops. This ends the refill.

3

Cut the fuel

Switch off the breaker on an electric heater, or turn a gas control knob to off. Never power an element in an empty tank.

4

Protect the area

Move electronics off the floor, set a bucket under any drip, and keep children and pets clear. Stay back from hot spray.

Leak triage

Read the puddle: where the water comes from decides the fix

Dry the floor, watch for a few minutes, and note where fresh drops form. That location tells us most of what we need before we open a toolbox.

What the leak location usually means
Where the water shows upLikely causeRepair or replace
Pipe connections on topLoose or corroded supply fittings, worn connectorsUsually repair
Pipe from the relief valveValve opening on high pressure or temperature, or a worn seatOften repair after a pressure test
Drain valve near the bottomWorn valve seat or debris stuck in itUsually repair
Electric element panelFailed element gasket or loose elementOften repair
Seam or base of the tankCorrosion has breached the steel shellReplace, since a shell cannot be patched

Condensation can fool you. A cold tank in a humid room can bead and drip with nothing wrong, so we dry every surface and see where moisture returns.

No heat

No hot water, lukewarm water, or a tank that runs out fast

Losing hot water rarely means the whole heater is finished. The symptom narrows the list of parts quickly.

Completely cold

Electric: tripped breaker, failed thermostat or burned-out element. Gas: pilot out or a flame sensor that no longer reads the flame.

Warm but never hot

One of two elements has failed, the thermostat is set low, or a gas burner is dirty.

Hot for a few minutes only

Sediment is eating tank volume, or a broken dip tube sends cold water straight to the outlet.

Rumbling or rusty water

Scale traps steam bubbles at the tank bottom, and rust-colored hot water points to the tank interior or a spent anode rod.

Fuel types

Gas, electric, and hybrid heaters fail in different ways

Gas units depend on a flame, a gas control valve, a flue and a flame sensor. We check venting and draft before calling the job done, and gas connections are licensed work, not DIY.

Electric units use two elements and two thermostats. We test each with a multimeter, and you should stay out of the access panel because the wiring is live.

Hybrid (heat pump) heaters add a compressor and fan above the tank. We diagnose the plumbing and electrical side and tell you early if a fault points to sealed refrigerant work, which falls outside plumbing.

Decision guide

Repair or replace: a quick way to decide

Repair makes sense when

  • Water comes from a fitting, valve, relief pipe or element gasket and the tank underneath is dry.
  • Testing finds a bad thermostat, element or pilot assembly.
  • The heater has had few problems so far.

Replace makes sense when

  • Water seeps from the tank seam or base.
  • Failures repeat and each fix buys only a few months.
  • The next repair costs about what a new installation would.

Manufacturers and plumbers commonly say many tank heaters reach their last years around a decade, but a well-kept unit may run longer. We decide from what the diagnosis shows, not from age alone.

Riverton homes

Local conditions that affect heaters here

Hard water and sediment

Riverton's 2022 water quality report lists average hardness of about 190 mg/L (roughly 11 grains per gallon). Scale builds faster in harder water, so ask about a yearly flush or a water softener.

Earthquake strapping

Utah's amendment (Utah Code 15A-3-305) calls for tank heaters to be strapped in the upper and lower third of their height. If yours has loose or missing straps, tell us during the visit.

City water, owner pipes

Riverton owns the line up to and including the meter. From the meter to your home is yours, so a pressure problem at the heater is checked on your side first.

Permit questions

Riverton requires a building permit for work on plumbing systems the code regulates. Ask the Riverton building department whether your repair needs one.
