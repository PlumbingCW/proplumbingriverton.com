Emergency

When a burst pipe is an emergency

Spraying water, a ceiling that sags, or water pouring from a wall is an emergency. So is any break you cannot stop. Even a steady drip from a split pipe can soak insulation and subfloor quickly, and our same-day and after-hours service exists for exactly this.

1

Close the main shutoff

Turn the valve where the water line enters the house clockwise. If it is stuck or leaks around the handle, say so when you call.

2

Drain the lines

Open the lowest faucet in the house, and a high one, to let remaining water out and ease pressure on the break.

3

Switch off the power

Cut the breaker for any room where water is near outlets, appliances or light fixtures. Stay out of standing water.

4

Turn off the water heater

With the supply closed, the heater should not run dry. Switch a gas unit to pilot or off, and cut the breaker on an electric one.

5

Photograph and move valuables

Capture the damage before cleanup, then lift rugs, electronics and paper above the wet area.

Freeze season

Frozen pipes: thaw safely or wait for us

Salt Lake City airport normals for 1991 to 2020 show about 112 days a year with a low at or below 32F, and a January normal low of 24.2F. Riverton runs a bit differently, but the pattern is the same: pipes in garages, outside walls and crawl areas are at risk from late fall through March.

Ice often swells a pipe and splits it without leaking, so the water only appears when the ice melts. That is why a thawing line can suddenly flood a room. If you can reach a frozen section, treat it as a possible break and keep the main valve within reach.

Safe while you wait

  • Open the faucet fed by the frozen line so melting water has an exit
  • Warm the room or aim a hair dryer on low at the pipe
  • Wrap the pipe with warm, damp towels
  • Check every faucet for a trickle or no flow

Never do this

  • Use a torch, lighter or any open flame
  • Keep heating a pipe that is bulging or already leaking
  • Thaw a line near gas piping
  • Ignore a trickle that turns to a drip behind a wall

Riverton's city code says the owner keeps service pipes in good repair and frost-protected at their own expense. In practice that means insulating exposed lines and keeping garage and outer-wall plumbing from freezing.

Where pipes fail

Why pipes crack, split or pinhole

Freeze splits

A long vertical split in a copper or PEX run, usually in an unheated wall or crawl area.

Pinholes in copper

Tiny spray from a spot in an aging line. It tends to repeat, so one hole often means more to come.

Cracked drain pipe

A crushed or cracked PVC drain pipe leaks when you use the sink or tub, and the water shows up below.

Joint and fitting failures

Soldered, crimped or glued connections can separate from vibration, movement or poor installation.

Pressure surges

A hammering line is a warning. Repeated spikes can fatigue weak pipe or loose fittings over time.

Riverton's 2022 water quality report lists average hardness around 190 mg/L, about 11 grains per gallon. Harder water leaves scale on the inside of fixtures, so ask us to look at the interior of any pipe we cut out. What we see tells you whether this was a one-off or a sign of aging lines.

Repair paths

How we repair a burst or cracked pipe

We start by finding the full extent of the damage, because a visible split can hide a longer crack. Then we cut back to sound pipe, which is the part many quick fixes skip.

Copper pipe repair

Copper repairs are soldered or made with push or press fittings once the line is dry. We dry the pipe, clean the ends, make the joint and pressure test it. If a pinhole shows up near an older repair, we talk about replacing a longer run so you are not chasing the next hole.

Drain pipe repair

A cracked drain pipe leaks only when it carries water, so the damage often shows after a shower or laundry load. We confirm the crack with a camera or a test run, then replace the damaged segment. Wider sewer concerns go to our sewer line repair team.

Before we arrive

Things to do while the plumber is on the way

1

Clear a path

Move boxes and stored items away from the main valve, the water heater and the wet area.

2

Mop up what you safely can

Towels and a wet vac help, but never use a standard vacuum on standing water.

3

Document for the claim

Take wide and close photos, note the time you found it, and keep any damaged section of pipe if we remove it.

4

Know your shutoffs

Tell us whether the main valve closes fully and whether a fixture shutoff is leaking. It affects our plan.

Homeowners policies often cover sudden burst-pipe damage but usually not slow leaks or wear, so call your insurer after the water is stopped. They may ask for photos, a written description of the repair and your plumber's findings, and we provide ours when you request them.

Hidden breaks

When the break is hidden or on the water line

If the meter keeps moving after the house shutoff is closed, the break is likely on the buried line, which belongs to the owner from the meter to the home. The city owns the line up to and including the meter. For a leak you cannot trace to a visible pipe, our leak detection service finds the spot before we dig.

The city says most spraying water calls turn out to be broken sprinkler lines, which you can stop at your property's ball valve. If water is erupting at the street side of the meter, call the city's after-hours water line. We handle everything from the meter to your home.
