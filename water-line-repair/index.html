Know the line

Who owns the water line in Riverton, and who fixes what

Riverton City is responsible for the water line on the street side up to and including the meter, which sits in the park strip. The property owner is responsible for the line from the meter to the home. That split decides who you call.

Which side of the meter has the problem
What you seeLikely sideFirst move
Water bubbling up at the street edge of the meterCity sideCall the city water line, do not dig
Soggy lawn between meter and house, meter moving with house shutoff closedOwner sideCall us, we locate then repair
Spraying water in a planter or lawnOften a sprinkler lineClose that line's ball valve, then call if it persists
No water or very low pressure at every tapEither sideCheck meter and shutoff, then call

The city does not diagnose responsibility by phone and needs an on-site look, so we treat the meter as the dividing line. After hours, the city lists on-call numbers of 801-558-1814 or 801-254-0704. For a major break or flooding during business hours, it lists 801-208-3113. Leave the meter box closed.

Emergency

When a water line break is an emergency

Water gushing in the yard, a sudden loss of pressure with a wet driveway, or a meter that spins with the house shut off all call for a fast response. We dispatch same-day when available, and after hours through the 24/7 line.

  • Close the main shutoff at the house to stop water flowing from the meter.
  • Keep kids and pets away from soft, undermined ground where a sinkhole could form.
  • Do not dig by hand or with equipment. Utilities may be buried nearby, and Blue Stakes asks you to call 811 first.
  • Run a faucet briefly to confirm the house is actually without water, so you can tell us what you see.
Symptoms

Signs the service line is leaking

Meter moves with everything off

Close the house shutoff and watch the dial. If it still moves, the leak is in the buried line.

Wet patch with no sprinkler

A green strip or soggy spot in dry weather may sit directly above the line.

Sudden pressure drop

A growing break pulls water out before it reaches the house.

Rushing sound near the meter

Listen at the shutoff or a hose bib while no fixtures run.

A bill that climbed

A steady leak adds up long before anything shows on the surface.

For leaks that are hard to pinpoint, we use acoustic listening and a line locator before any digging begins. Our leak detection page explains the instruments. We never guess where to open the ground.

Dig or not

Trench repair, trenchless and replacement

Once the leak is located, the choice is how much pipe to replace and how to reach it. The right answer depends on the condition of the line, what sits above it and where it enters the house.

Open trench

  • Fastest path to a visible, tested repair
  • Lets us replace a short bad section or the whole run
  • Lawn, concrete or landscaping must be restored
  • Best when the break is shallow and accessible

Trenchless

  • Fewer holes, less damage to driveways and landscaping
  • Suits long runs under hardscape
  • Needs a pipe path that can be pulled or burst
  • Not every line or soil condition allows it

A spot repair makes sense if the pipe is otherwise sound and the break is a single failed joint or crush. If the line shows repeated leaks, corrosion or poor material, replacing the run is cheaper over time than reopening the same yard. Water main replacement from the street is city work, so we stop at the meter.

Before any excavation, we call Blue Stakes at 811. Notice must be given before digging, and Blue Stakes recommends allowing several business days, so we plan the schedule around the ticket.

Low pressure

Low water pressure: is it the line or the house?

Low pressure does not always mean a broken service line. It can come from a partly closed valve, clogged aerators, a failing pressure regulator if the home has one, or a leak somewhere along the line. The tests are simple, and they save you from paying for the wrong repair.

1

Compare hot and cold

If only hot water is weak, look at the water heater, not the service line.

2

Test several fixtures

One weak faucet usually means a clogged aerator or cartridge. All fixtures weak means the problem is upstream.

3

Read a gauge at the hose bib

We attach a pressure gauge to an outdoor spigot, read static pressure, then open a fixture and watch how far it drops.

4

Check the meter

A moving dial with the house closed turns a pressure complaint into a leak search.

Old corroded steel lines can also lose flow as they scale inside. If the service line is metal and failing across its length, consider our repiping page, because a patched section may not fix the whole flow problem.

Backflow

Backflow assemblies and your water line

Backflow assemblies keep irrigation or other water from flowing backward into the drinking supply. According to the city's backflow program, assemblies are tested when installed, repaired or relocated, then yearly by a certified tester, paid for by the owner, with results reported to the city.

If your irrigation connects to the culinary line, the city says it needs a reduced pressure principle assembly, often called an RPZ. A line repair that disturbs the assembly may trigger a retest, so tell us whether your yard has one. Ask when you call whether we can handle the testing for you.
