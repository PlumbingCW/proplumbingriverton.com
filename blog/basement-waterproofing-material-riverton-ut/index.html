Basement Waterproofing Materials: What Works for What in Riverton, UT

The right waterproofing material depends on where the water comes from. Surface coatings handle damp walls, interior drains and a sump pump handle water that rises from below, and none of them fix a leaking pipe.

Start with the source. Then pick the material.

By the Riverton Pro Plumbing team · Published October 29, 2026 · Updated October 29, 2026

Before you buy anything

Four sources of basement water, and why the material depends on which one you have

Basement water usually comes from one of four places: groundwater pressing on the foundation, surface water running toward the house, a plumbing leak, or condensation on cold surfaces. A material that suits one will do little for the others.

A common mistake is to seal a wall that is wet because of a pipe or a gutter. The sealer holds for a while, then the water finds another way in. Diagnosis comes first, and it can be as simple as looking at when the water appears.

How to tell the sources apart
ClueLikely sourceWhere to look
Wet only after rain or snowmeltSurface water or groundwaterGrading, downspouts, window wells, floor-wall joint
Wet all the time, even in dry weeksPlumbing leakWater meter test, supply lines, drain lines, water heater
Damp patches on cold walls in humid weatherCondensationCold pipes, uninsulated walls, poor ventilation
Water rising through the floor slab edgeGroundwater pressureFloor-wall joint, cracks, sump pit
Wet near one fixture or wall onlyPipe or fixtureSupply and drain lines behind that wall

You can run a simple meter test yourself. Shut off every water use in the house, then watch the water meter dial. If it moves, water is going somewhere. A plumber can locate the leak with leak detection tools before any waterproofing money is spent.

The materials

What each waterproofing material actually does

Masonry sealers and coatings

Brushed or rolled onto interior walls to slow moisture vapor and light dampness. They do not stop water under pressure, and a heavy coating over active leaks can peel.

Hydraulic cement

A fast-setting cement used to plug small cracks and holes. Useful for a defined crack, but it can fail if the wall keeps moving.

Interior drain channel

A channel along the floor edge collects water that enters at the wall-floor joint and sends it to a sump. It manages water, it does not keep it out.

Sump basin and pump

A pit in the floor collects water and a pump sends it outside. This is where our sump pump service comes in, including battery backup and discharge routing.

Wall drainage panels

Dimpled sheets on the wall let moisture drain down to the channel instead of soaking the wall. They are paired with a drain system and a floor-level exit.

Window well drains and covers

Wells that fill with water can leak through the window. A drain at the bottom and a cover keep water from collecting against the glass.

What Riverton homeowners should know

The Utah Geological Survey's Special Study 152 covers the Copperton quadrangle, which includes parts of Riverton, South Jordan, West Jordan and Herriman. It maps shallow groundwater, which the study says can flood basements. It also maps collapsible soils that lose volume when wet, and expansive soils that swell when wet and shrink when dry, which can crack foundations and damage underground utilities.

The study does not say which Riverton blocks fall in the mapped part. So check your own property, watch for seasonal rises in water at the floor edge, and ask a plumber to look at your sump and drains. Do not assume your home is affected, and do not assume it is not.

Sources: Utah Geological Survey Special Study 152, Geologic Hazards of the Copperton Quadrangle

Know the scope

What a plumber handles and what needs someone else

Plumber scope

  • Finding and fixing leaking supply and drain lines
  • Installing and servicing sump pumps and discharge piping
  • Checking sewer backups and floor drains
  • Moving water from basin to a legal discharge point, with local rules confirmed
  • Diagnosing whether the water is plumbing or seepage

Other trades

  • Foundation crack repair and structural work
  • Exterior excavation and membranes on foundation walls
  • Regrading the yard and fixing gutters
  • Concrete patching beyond small cracks
  • Anything that changes the structure of the house

We say this plainly because a waterproofing quote that bundles everything under one price can hide what is plumbing and what is not. Our basement waterproofing service stays within plumbing scope, and we will tell you when the fix is outside it.

Matching the fix

A decision path from symptom to material

1

Rule out plumbing first

Run the meter test and check the water heater, supply lines and fixture drains. A plumbing leak ends the waterproofing question.

2

Check the outside

Look at downspouts, gutters, grading and window wells. Moving water away from the house costs less than managing it inside.

3

Identify seepage versus pressure

Dampness that dries between rains calls for a coating or wall panel. Pooling water at the floor edge calls for a drain channel and sump.

4

Size the sump to the problem

A basin and pump should fit the volume of water you see, with a backup for power outages.

5

Confirm the discharge

Ask the building department about discharge rules before water goes to the yard or street.

Common mistakes

Five ways waterproofing money gets wasted

  • ✓Painting over an active leak.The coating blisters and the wall stays wet. Fix the source first.
  • ✓Skipping the backup.A sump pump fails during a storm power outage. A battery backup or alarm helps.
  • ✓Piping the discharge too close to the house.Water that comes out near the foundation can return. Route it well away, after confirming the rules.
  • ✓Ignoring condensation.A dehumidifier and insulation on cold pipes may solve what a membrane never will.
  • ✓Buying a material before a diagnosis.The wrong product costs money and leaves the problem in place.

Water that seeps in is not always a basement problem. If you find standing water around a floor drain, a toilet base, or a washing machine, read about leak detection first, because those are plumbing symptoms.
