How to Keep Drains From Clogging in Your Riverton, UT Home

Most household clogs are built slowly from grease, hair, soap film, paper and things that should have gone in the trash. Keeping drains clear comes down to controlling what enters each room's drain and doing a few small checks on a schedule.

Below is a room-by-room plan, a maintenance calendar, and a plain comparison of when a plunger, a cable machine or hydro jetting fits the problem.

KitchenBathroomLaundryMain line

By the Riverton Pro Plumbing team · Published October 29, 2026 · Updated October 29, 2026

Kitchen

The kitchen drain: grease is the real enemy

Hot grease looks like a liquid going down the drain. A few feet later it cools, sticks to the pipe wall, and starts catching coffee grounds, rice and food scraps. Over months it forms a hard, waxy lining that narrows the pipe.

Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel and put the towel in the trash. Pour cooled cooking fat into a can or jar, and throw that away. A garbage disposal grinds food, but it does not stop grease from coating the line beyond it.

  • ✓Scrape plates into the trashSolid scraps belong in the bin, not in the sink basket.
  • ✓Skip the starchy and stringy itemsPasta, rice, potato peels, celery and corn husks swell or tangle in the trap and the disposal.
  • ✓Keep coffee grounds outGrounds settle in the bottom of horizontal pipes and pack together.
  • ✓Run cold water with the disposalCold water keeps fats firmer so the blades chop them rather than smearing them.
  • ✓Flush with hot water after the dishesA good flush of hot tap water moves loose residue past the trap before it settles.
Bathroom

Bathroom sinks, tubs and toilets clog for different reasons

Bathroom clog sources and the simple habit that prevents each
FixtureWhat builds upHabit that helps
Shower and tub drainHair wrapped in soap scumFit a mesh catcher and empty it weekly
Bathroom sinkToothpaste, hair and soap film around the stopperPull the stopper monthly and clean it
ToiletToo much paper or flushed items that do not break downFlush only toilet paper and waste
Floor drain in a basement bathroomDried-out trap and lintPour water into it every few weeks

Toilets are built for human waste and toilet paper. Diapers, paper towels, cotton balls, dental floss and the so-called flushable products all tend to snag in a bend and build a plug. If it did not come out of your body or off the roll, put it in a wastebasket.

Hair is the leading cause of slow showers. A cheap catcher over the drain stops it before it forms a mat that clings to the pipe wall.

Laundry and floors

Laundry, utility sinks and floor drains

A washing machine sends a lot of water, lint and detergent residue into a standpipe in a short time. A lint trap on the discharge hose catches fibers before they reach the line. Clean it every few loads, because a clogged trap or crushed hose can back water out of the standpipe.

Utility sinks catch paint, plaster and grit that should never go down a drain. Wash brushes in a bucket, let solids settle, and dispose of the sludge in the trash. Plaster and joint compound harden inside a trap like cement.

Floor drains in garages and basements often sit unused for months. Their traps dry out and let sewer gas rise, and debris collects around the grate. Pour a pitcher of water into each one every few weeks to refill the trap.

What Riverton homeowners should know

Riverton's sewer provider, Jordan Basin Improvement District (formerly South Valley Sewer District), says not to pour cooking grease down drains, even if you have a garbage disposal. Put it in the trash instead. The district also asks residents not to flush diapers or other disposable hygiene products. Roots, grease and flushed objects are among the common causes of sewer problems.

The district maintains the main, and the private line from your house to the main is yours. Its cleanout test is simple: if you open the cleanout and see standing water, a blockage is likely between the house and the main, so call the district at (801) 571-1166. If the cleanout is dry, the problem is probably in your home plumbing.

Sources: Jordan Basin Improvement District (JBID) sewer service guidance

Safety first

What to do and what not to do with a slow drain

  • ✓Do not pour chemical openers in a standing drain.They sit in the pipe, can damage fixtures and are dangerous if a plumber later opens the line.
  • ✓Do not mix cleaners.Combining products can release harmful fumes, so use one product at a time, in a ventilated room.
  • ✓Do use a plunger or a hand auger first.Both are safe for a fixture clog and tell you whether the blockage is near the drain.
  • ✓Call if several drains slow at once.That pattern points to the main line, and a plumber with a camera is the safe next step.

Slow drains in more than one room?

A simple maintenance schedule that actually gets done

Skip the bottles of caustic drain opener. They can sit in a stopped pipe and eat at the fixture and the joints, and they leave a hazard for the plumber who has to open the line. A plunger and a small hand auger, used gently, handle most minor clogs.

Tools

Plunger, cable or jet: matching the fix to the clog

Not every clog needs the heaviest tool. Match the method to the problem and you avoid both wasted effort and unneeded damage.

A plunger clears soft blockages at one fixture. A hand auger or a cable machine breaks through a plug in a branch line. When the pipe wall itself is coated with grease or scale, a cable only drills a hole through the middle, and the buildup comes back.

Cabling or snaking

  • Opens a single blockage by boring through it
  • Good for hair, paper and a localized obstruction
  • Leaves the pipe wall coating in place
  • Often the first and cheapest step for one stuck drain

Hydro jetting

  • Scours the full pipe wall with high-pressure water
  • Suits recurring clogs, grease coating and fine roots
  • Should follow a camera look at the pipe's condition
  • Not suited to a pipe that is cracked or collapsing

If you have cleared the same drain more than twice in a season, the pipe likely has a coating or a defect that more plunging will not fix. A professional drain cleaning visit, with a camera if needed, finds out why instead of treating the same symptom again.

Warning signs

When a clog is no longer a household problem

  • ✓Several fixtures back up at once.Water from the washer appearing in a tub, or a toilet that bubbles when the shower runs, points at the main line.
  • ✓Sewage rises in the lowest drain.That is a full blockage downstream. Keep people and pets away and run no water.
  • ✓Drains clear and then slow again within weeks.Coating, roots or a sag in the pipe is more likely than one stray obstruction.
  • ✓A gurgling sound after flushing.Air is pushing through a partly blocked line or a vent that is not working properly.

We also dispatch to Draper and Bluffdale, and the advice is the same there: a slow drain ignored for months is easier to deal with than a backup at midnight, so get an early camera look.

Rules and sources

Rules and sources behind this page

The guidance on grease and flushing comes from Jordan Basin Improvement District, which asks customers not to pour cooking grease down drains and not to flush diapers or other disposable hygiene products.

Drain and trap work follows the plumbing code Utah adopts. Utah Code 15A-2-103 adopts the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as the state plumbing code, effective July 1, 2026, with Utah amendments on top.

Drain clog prevention questions

Grease and food residue that cool and stick to the pipe wall. A disposal does not prevent that. Scrape pans into the trash and put cooled fat in a container.

Clean catchers and stoppers weekly or monthly, flush kitchen drains with hot water regularly, and check the cleanout once a year. A professional cleaning makes sense when a drain keeps slowing down after you have cleared it.

Hair and soap scum build up deeper in the line than you can reach. A cable machine clears the plug, and if it returns quickly, a camera look shows whether the pipe is coated or damaged.
