Water Heater Installation in Riverton, UT

Water heater installation in Riverton, UT is the planned work of setting a new heater, whether it is for a remodel, a new build or an upgrade you scheduled ahead of a failure.

Planning gives you room to choose the type, location and connections without a cold shower hanging over the decision. If your heater has already failed, replacement is the faster path.

The package

What gets installed along with the heater

A heater is only part of the job. The pieces around it decide whether it runs safely for years or leaks in a closet nobody checks.

Drip pan

Catches slow leaks and gives a failing tank somewhere to drain besides your floor. Utah's amendment says a heater pan may receive discharge from the heater only.

Relief valve and discharge pipe

The safety valve opens on excess pressure or temperature, and its pipe must send hot water somewhere safe.

Expansion tank

Some systems need one to absorb pressure swings. A plumber confirms whether yours does, and we do not assume.

Shutoff valves and connectors

A working shutoff above the heater makes every future repair easier.

Gas line, flue and venting

Gas heaters need a leak-tested connection and a flue that draws properly. This is licensed work.

Seismic straps

Utah's code amendment calls for straps in the upper and lower third of the tank height.

Placement

Where the heater goes matters more than most people expect

Moving a heater adds pipework, venting or wiring, so many homeowners keep it where it is. If you are remodeling, a better spot may be worth the extra work.

  • Leave clear access in front of the tank for service and for pulling an element or burner assembly.
  • Keep combustibles away from a gas heater and give it the air it needs to burn properly.
  • Think about where water would go if the tank failed, then put the pan and a floor drain, if you have one, in that path.
  • A shorter run to the kitchen and bathrooms means hot water arrives sooner and less water goes down the drain waiting.

Clearances depend on the unit and the code, so we confirm them for your model instead of quoting a single number.

Safety first

What to do to prepare for an install day

  • ✓Clear the area.Move storage, paint cans and anything flammable away from the heater and the work path.
  • ✓Know your shutoffs.Find the main water valve and the gas shutoff before the plumber arrives, and tell the plumber where they are.
  • ✓Do not run hot taps during the swap.Water flowing while the heater is drained can scald or flood the area.

Install day

How an install day runs

1

Walk-through

We confirm the old unit, the fuel, the vent path and where the new heater, pan and straps will sit.

2

Shut down and remove

Water, power and gas come off, the tank is drained and the old unit comes out.

3

Set, strap and pipe

The new heater goes on its pan, gets strapped, and receives new connectors, shutoffs and a relief valve line.

4

Gas, vent and wiring

Gas connections get a leak test, the flue is checked for draft, and electric units get a verified circuit.

5

Fill, test and explain

We fill the tank, check every joint, set the temperature and show you the shutoffs and the relief valve.

6

Haul-away

The old heater leaves with us. We do not promise a clock time, because venting and access change the day.

Planned or emergency

Why a scheduled install is better than a rushed one

Planned installation

  • You choose the heater type and size with time to compare.
  • Gas, vent and wiring upgrades are scoped before the day.
  • You can ask the building department about permits first.
  • Parts are ready and nobody is without hot water.

Emergency swap

  • The choice is usually a like-for-like unit.
  • Hidden problems, such as old vents, show up mid-job.
  • Permit questions get squeezed into the same day.
  • Floors and walls may already be wet.

If you are considering a tankless unit, a planned install is much easier, since gas line size and vent routing can be checked ahead of time.

Rules and permits

Code, permit and local water notes

Plumbing code

Utah Code 15A-2-103 adopts the 2024 International Plumbing Code effective 7/1/2026, with Utah amendments on strapping and pans. Your plumber works to that code.

Permit question

Riverton requires a building permit for work on plumbing and gas systems the code regulates, via building@rivertonutah.gov. Ask the Riverton building department what a heater install needs.

Hard water

Riverton's 2022 water quality report lists average hardness of about 190 mg/L (about 11 grains per gallon). Scale builds faster in harder water, so ask us about flushing or a softener.

License check

You can verify any plumber on the Utah Division of Professional Licensing lookup (secure.utah.gov/llv/search/index.html), profession PLUMBER.

Rules and sources behind this page
