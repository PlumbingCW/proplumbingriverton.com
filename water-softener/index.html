Step one

Test your tap before you buy anything

1

Read the city report

Ask the Riverton water division for its annual water quality report. The 2022 report lists a hardness average of about 190 mg/L with a range of 174.5 to 202.5.

2

Look for the symptoms

White crust on faucets and showerheads, spots on glass, stiff laundry and scale in the kettle.

3

Test at your own tap

A home test strip or a lab test gives your number, since pipes and any existing softener change what reaches you.

4

Decide what you want solved

Hardness, chlorine taste, or a drinking-water concern each call for a different tool.

Riverton history

Old settings can waste salt

A notice from the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District to Riverton residents says hardness moved from about 30 grains per gallon to about 12 after the 2015 switch to JVWCD water. It adds that softeners set for the old water waste salt.

If your softener was installed before that change and nobody has reset it, the hardness setting may be far too high. Resetting the control valve to match today's water is quick work, and it cuts salt use and the brine going down your drain.

Regeneration flushes brine to the drain, so a setting that is higher than you need sends salt down the line with no benefit. We set the unit to the number your test shows.

Choosing

Softener, filter or reverse osmosis: what each one does

Water treatment options compared
OptionWhat it doesWhat it does not do
Salt-based softenerRemoves hardness minerals from all water in the houseDoes not remove chlorine taste or most contaminants
Carbon or sediment filterCuts chlorine taste, odor and particlesDoes not soften water
Reverse osmosis at one tapTreats drinking and cooking water finelyTreats one tap only and wastes some water
Softener plus filterSoft water through the home, better-tasting water at the kitchen tapCosts more and needs two sets of maintenance

Our usual advice is to solve the problem you can see or taste, not to buy the largest system. Chlorine is listed as the drinking water disinfectant in Riverton's 2022 report, so a kitchen filter may handle the taste issue alone.

When a softener stops working right

Hard water comes back

Spent resin, a stuck valve, an empty salt tank or a bridged salt crust can all stop regeneration.

Salt level never drops

A cycle that is not running, or a salt bridge that keeps the brine from forming.

Water runs to drain constantly

A worn control valve or a stuck piston leaves the unit cycling.

Loud cycling or odd noises

Often a failing motor, a clogged injector or a pressure issue.

Valve and head replacement is often cheaper than a whole new system, but resin that is many years old may be worth replacing along with it. We tell you which parts have failed before we quote.

Is it worth it?

Not every home needs a softener

A softener tends to pay off if

  • Scale is building on fixtures, in the water heater or in appliances.
  • You are tired of spots and stiff laundry.
  • Your own test confirms hard water.

Skip it or use a filter if

  • Your complaint is taste or smell, not scale.
  • Your test shows moderate hardness and you are not bothered.
  • You cannot maintain salt and the unit.

Scale can shorten the life of heaters. Our heater repair and tankless service visits see this scale often. New softeners are plumbed in by a licensed plumber, and a larger plumbing installation can include it.
