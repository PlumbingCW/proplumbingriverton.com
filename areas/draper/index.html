Historic streets

Fort Street, 700 East and 13200 South: Draper's oldest homes

Draper's own historic preservation page says the city's historic resources are few, dwindling, and found on only a few small streets. The homes it documents were built between 1853 and 1899, on Fort Street, 700 East and 13200 South. The same page lists the Day Dairy Barn and Draper Park School among the historic sites.

A house that old has almost certainly had its plumbing changed more than once. Age alone does not tell us what is inside the walls, so we look instead of assuming. A camera run on the sewer line and a pressure check on the supply show whether repiping or a spot fix is the honest answer.

Whether any preservation rules touch a plumbing job at your address is a question for Draper's historic preservation program. Ask before you plan a trench across the front yard.

Two systems

Two water systems, two sets of rules

Draper runs two separate water systems. Draper City Water delivers drinking water and buys from Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District. WaterPro and Draper Irrigation Company run the secondary irrigation system, whose season is roughly mid April to mid October.

That split matters when something leaks. A wet spot that appears in January is on the drinking-water side, because irrigation lines sit empty out of season. A wet spot in July could be either one, so find out which system it belongs to before you dig.

We trace and repair the private side of your plumbing, and we tell you plainly when the fault is on the utility side. For a buried supply line, that is water line repair.

Draper's backflow administrator inspects commercial buildings and residential irrigation systems, and assemblies must be tested every year. If your sprinkler system has a backflow device, the annual test is your responsibility, so put it on the fall checklist.

Parks and trails

Parks, trails and Corner Canyon: outdoor plumbing

The city counts more than 42 parks, over 150 miles of trails and 5,000 acres of open space, and Corner Canyon is its named trail system. If your lot backs onto open space, you may have more outdoor plumbing to protect than a typical lot, including spigots and irrigation zones.

Every outdoor spigot is a place that can freeze. A bib that splits in the first hard cold snap becomes a burst pipe problem once water gets into the wall. Remove hoses and close the interior shutoff for each outdoor line before the freeze.

What we know about water, sewer and ground in Draper

Drinking water

The 2023 report from Draper City Water lists groundwater and surface sources, plus purchases from the district. The copy we read did not list hardness, so test your tap before you size a softener.

Irrigation

The season runs about April 15 to October 15 on the secondary system. Plan the fall blowout for when it ends and the spring check for startup.

Sewer

Jordan Basin Improvement District serves Draper. It maintains the main, and the private line from your house to the main is yours. Call the district when water stands at the cleanout.

Ground

Draper's December 2025 geotechnical standards require testing for compressibility, collapse potential and groundwater depth. That is a rule for builders, not a finding about your lot, but a newer home may have a geotechnical report. Ask for it before basement work.

The district says clay sewer pipes from before the 1980s are prone to root intrusion. Wherever your lateral runs, a camera look is the way to know. A section that is cracked or displaced calls for sewer line repair, while roots alone may only need clearing.
