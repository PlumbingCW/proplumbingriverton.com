Growth

From 21,785 residents to about 55,000: what growth means under the floor

Herriman counted 21,785 residents in 2010, and the 2020 Census counted about 55,000. That climb explains most of the calls we take here: new lines for finished basements, added bathrooms, detached garages and shops, and water heaters bought at the same time.

In a city that adds homes quickly, a lot of work is installation, not repair. That is why we lead with plumbing installation on this page, and why permits matter more here than they might elsewhere.

Fast growth also means new neighbors on every side, and not every street shares the same water arrangement. If you are not sure whether your address is on the city's secondary system or which water report applies to you, check the city's water page before you call about a water problem.

Permits

Adding or replacing: what Herriman's building department asks for

Herriman's building department says a permit is required to replace or add a water heater and to add new plumbing lines to a house or a detached structure. Repair and maintenance of existing plumbing does not need one. Plan around that line before you start a remodel.

Water

Wells in summer, JVWCD in winter: why your water can change

Herriman's 2025 water report lists five wells, one spring, and purchases from Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District and from Bluffdale City. Since fall 2024, the city says it uses only JVWCD water from October to April and local wells in summer.

Herriman's FAQ says JVWCD water is less hard than water from local sources, but it gives no hardness number. Scale on fixtures and in heaters may vary by season. Test your tap in July and again in January before you size a softener or choose a conditioning system.

Yard side

Secondary water, hose bibs and the backflow rules

Secondary water in Herriman is metered, available only in areas on the city's map, and runs from about May 1 to mid-October. It is untreated, and the city filters it to 200 microns. Treat it as irrigation only and keep it away from anything connected to drinking water.

Mechanical backflow preventers must be tested within 10 days of first use and then every year, and hose bibs need vacuum breakers. A weeping hose bib or a broken yard line is a job for a water line repair visit, and the preventer gets retested after any work.
