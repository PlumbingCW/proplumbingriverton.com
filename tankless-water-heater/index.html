Tankless Water Heater Service in Riverton, UT

A tankless water heater heats water only when a tap opens, so there is no stored tank to run out or to rupture. In Riverton, UT the real questions are gas line size, venting, and how hard water treats the heat exchanger over time.

We install tankless units, flush and descale them, and repair what we can. Some companies will replace a tankless heater but not service it, which is a gap we fill.

Fit check

Does a tankless heater suit your house?

Tankless tends to suit you if

  • You want hot water without waiting for a tank to recover between showers.
  • You have wall space and a safe way to vent a gas unit.
  • Your gas line can carry the load, or you are willing to upgrade it.
  • You plan to stay long enough to appreciate the savings and maintain the unit.

A tank may suit you better if

  • Your gas line or venting would need major changes.
  • You want the lowest upfront cost and the simplest swap.
  • You do not want a yearly flush on your schedule.
  • Several fixtures run at once and the unit would be undersized.

We size the unit by how many fixtures run together and the temperature rise your incoming water needs. We do not size it by guesswork or by house size alone. If you are replacing a failed tank, our replacement page explains the tank route.

Infrastructure

Gas line, venting and power come before the heater

A tankless gas unit burns a lot of gas in a short burst. A pipe that served a tank heater fine can starve a tankless unit, and the symptom is a heater that shuts down or runs cold under load.

  • ✓Gas supply.We measure the existing line run and fittings against the unit's input rating, and upgrade where it falls short.
  • ✓Venting.These units need their own approved vent materials and a clear route to the outdoors. Tank flues cannot simply be reused.
  • ✓Electric and condensate.Most units need a power outlet, and condensing models need a drain for acidic condensate.
  • ✓Water pipe sizing.Undersized hot water lines cut flow, so we check pipe size and shutoffs, including isolation valves for later flushing.

Gas connections and venting are licensed work. If you ever smell gas, leave and call Enbridge Gas Utah at 1-800-767-1689 or 911 before calling us.

Maintenance

Flushing and descaling a tankless heater

Riverton's 2022 water quality report lists average hardness of about 190 mg/L, roughly 11 grains per gallon. Scale builds faster in harder water, and a tankless heat exchanger has very narrow passages that scale can narrow further.

Signs it is time for service include an error code, lukewarm water that used to run hot, a falling flow rate, or noises from the unit. Many callers simply notice the heater has gone years without attention.

1

Isolate the unit

Close the water and gas, and open the service valves.

2

Circulate descaler

A pump pushes a mild acid solution through the heat exchanger for a set period.

3

Rinse

We flush with clean water until it runs clear, then remove the filter screen.

4

Clean the inlet screen

Debris on the screen lowers flow, so we clean it every visit.

5

Test and record

We restore gas and water, run hot water, and check for error codes.

If your tap tests hard, a water softener ahead of the heater reduces scale at the source.

Safety first

What to do if a tankless unit acts up

  • ✓Smell gas? Leave first.Walk out and call Enbridge Gas Utah at 1-800-767-1689 or 911 from outside. Do not use switches or phones in the building.
  • ✓Turn off power at the unit.Electric and gas units both have controls. Switch them off before checking anything near the vent or the water connections.
  • ✓Do not touch hot vent pipes.Exhaust pipes run hot. Keep children and pets away from them.
  • ✓Close the cold supply for a leak.Water on the floor under the unit means shut the cold inlet and call.
