What to do while a drain is backing up

Standing water in a tub or a gurgling toilet usually means the blockage sits past the fixture. Every flush and every load of laundry adds to the pile in the pipe, so stop using water until the line is open.

Move rugs and cardboard boxes off wet floors. Keep kids and pets away from water that came up through a floor drain, because that water can carry sewage.

  • ✓Skip the chemical openers.They sit on top of a standing clog, can burn skin, and splash back at whoever works on the pipe next.
  • ✓Don't plunge a toilet that is also backing into the tub.The pressure just pushes water to the weakest fixture.
  • ✓Write down which fixtures are slow.That one detail tells us whether the clog is in a branch line or in the line that serves the whole house.
Find the clog

Fixture line or main line? How to tell

One slow bathroom sink is almost always a branch line problem. When the toilet, shower and washing machine drain all misbehave together, the blockage is probably downstream in the building drain that carries everything out of the house.

Cable work

Snaking and cabling: what the machine does in the pipe

1

Pick the access point

A sink trap, a tub overflow, a toilet pulled from its flange, or the cleanout. The nearest access to the clog keeps the cable short and the cleanup small.

2

Run the cable

A motorized cable machine feeds a flexible steel cable into the line. The operator feels for the point where it slows or stops.

3

Cut or retrieve

A spear head breaks soft stoppages. A blade head cuts through roots. A retrieving head pulls out hair, wipes and other debris.

4

Flush and test

We run water through the line and watch the drain. A fast, quiet flow is the goal, not just a hole in the clog.

Cabling clears the center of a pipe. It does not scrape the walls clean. If the cable stops at the same distance every time, or the drain slows again within weeks, a camera look or hydro jetting is the better next step.

Kitchen

Kitchen sink clogs and the grease problem

Kitchen lines clog differently from bathroom lines. Cooking grease goes down warm and liquid, then cools into a waxy coating that catches coffee grounds and food scraps until the pipe narrows to a trickle.

JBID's guidance is plain: don't pour cooking grease down the drain, even if you have a disposal. Let it cool and put it in the trash. A disposal chops food, but it does nothing about fat.

  • Check whether the dishwasher drain hose empties into the disposal, because a clog there backs dirty water into the dishwasher.
  • If the disposal hums but won't spin, that is a motor jam, not a drain clog. See our garbage disposal repair page.
  • Water that rises in the second sink bowl when the first drains points to a clog past the tee, not at the strainer.

Riverton specifics

What shapes drain problems in Riverton homes

The sewer main belongs to JBID

The district maintains the main. The private line from your house to the main is yours, so a clog past the cleanout is often a district call and a clog before it is a plumber call.

Clay pipes and roots

JBID notes that clay sewer pipes from before the 1980s are prone to root intrusion. If your lateral is that old and clogs repeatedly, ask for a camera look before paying for another cable pass.

Hard water leaves scale

Riverton's 2022 water quality report lists average hardness of about 190 mg/L, roughly 11 grains per gallon. Scale can narrow older metal drain and supply pipes over time, so slow drains in aging plumbing deserve a closer look.

Flushed items count

JBID asks residents not to flush diapers or other disposable hygiene products. They don't break down, so they catch in lateral joints and build a plug.

Most sewer-side problems are covered on our sewer line cleaning page, and we also dispatch to South Jordan and Herriman from our Riverton base.

Hidden damage

When a clog is hiding a drain line leak

Some clogs come with a second problem. A damp cabinet floor, a musty smell under a sink, or a ceiling stain below a bathroom can mean a drain pipe that has cracked or separated at a joint.

We run a camera or a water test to confirm before anyone opens a wall, and a sewer camera inspection covers the longer runs. If the line is damaged, cabling alone will not fix it.
