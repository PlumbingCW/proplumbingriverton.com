Emergency Sump Pump Repair in Riverton, UT

A sump pump collects groundwater in a pit and pushes it out of the house before it reaches the floor. In Riverton, UT, a pump that stops running while water rises in the pit is an emergency, and our 24/7 line is staffed by licensed plumbers.

Water rising

Pump failed and water is rising: do this first

1

Stay out of standing water

Water on a basement floor can reach outlets, appliances and the pump cord. If water is near electrical items, leave the area and shut off power from a dry location only.

2

Check the simple causes

Look for a tripped GFCI outlet, an unplugged cord and a float stuck against the pit wall. Reset or free them if you can do so without stepping in water.

3

Buy time

A wet vacuum or bucket brigade at the pit slows the rise. Lift stored items off the floor.

4

Note where it enters

Water bubbling up through the pit is groundwater. Water seeping along a wall may be a different leak. Tell us which you see.

How it works

What is inside a sump system

A sump system has four parts. The pit holds water and the pump moves it. A float switch tells the pump when to start. A discharge pipe with a check valve carries the water outside and stops it from falling back in.

Local ground

Why groundwater matters in Riverton

The Utah Geological Survey's Special Study 152 maps shallow groundwater in the Copperton quadrangle, which includes parts of Riverton, South Jordan, West Jordan and Herriman. The study says shallow groundwater can flood basements and other underground spaces. It does not say which Riverton blocks fall in the mapped part.

The practical lesson is simple. Do not assume your basement is safe because it has never flooded. Groundwater can rise with the season, so a pit that sat dry all winter may fill in spring. If you own a home with a basement or sunken lower level, check the pump before the wet months.

We do not claim that every Riverton basement needs a pump. We look at the pit, the water and the signs of past moisture, then tell you if a pump is warranted.

Diagnosing a pump that fails

Runs constantly

A stuck float, a failed check valve that lets water drain back, or a pit that cannot keep up with inflow.

Hums but does not pump

A jammed impeller or a failing motor. Debris or silt is often the culprit.

Will not start

No power, a tripped GFCI, a dead float switch or a burnt-out motor.

Starts and stops rapidly

A pit that is too small, a float set wrong or a check valve allowing backflow.

Loud rattle or vibration

A worn bearing or a loose discharge pipe. Both usually show up before total failure.

We pull the pump, test it, clean the pit and check the float and valve. If the pump is worn, replacement is usually more honest than another repair.

Installing a new pit or a backup pump

A new installation means breaking a section of slab, setting the pit liner, placing the pump and running a discharge pipe outside. The pipe should carry water away from the foundation, not toward the neighbor's yard or the street without a plan.

Discharge rules vary, so ask the Riverton building department what applies before you choose a route. If the work involves new plumbing lines, the city says a building permit is required for plumbing systems the code regulates.
