The valve will not shut off: what to do right now

Work upstream until the water stops. Each fixture has its own stop, then the house has a main. If one of them fails, you move one step closer to the street.

  • ✓Fixture stop.Under a sink, look for two small oval or round handles on the pipes feeding the faucet. Turn them clockwise.
  • ✓Main shutoff.Usually where the water line enters the home, often in a basement, garage or utility room. A ball-style lever turns a quarter turn. A round wheel handle needs several turns.
  • ✓If the main will not close.Do not force it. Open a low faucet to drain pressure, and call us. A seized main valve is a job for a plumber, not a pipe wrench.
  • ✓Street side.Riverton City owns the water line up to and including the meter. The owner is responsible from the meter to the home. Do not open the meter box yourself.

Sometimes a handle spins freely and the water keeps coming. That usually means the stem or cartridge inside has stripped, and the stop under the sink is the quickest fix. Mop up standing water, then call us.

Faucet types

Four common faucet designs and their weak points

Compression

Separate hot and cold handles. A rubber washer wears against a seat. Cheap to fix, but old seats can be pitted.

Cartridge

One or two handles over a sealed insert. Leaks start as a drip or a stiff, gritty handle. The insert swaps out.

Ball

Single lever over a slotted ball with rubber seals. Seals and springs go first, and the leak shows at the base.

Ceramic disc

Smooth, quiet handles. Mineral grit can score the discs, causing slow drips. A cleaned or new cylinder fixes it.

Matching the exact replacement part matters. Parts that almost fit leak again in a month, which is why we pull the old one out and compare before buying anything.

Bathroom

Shower valves, showerheads and the tub faucet

A showerhead that drips is usually a worn washer or scale in the arm, and it unscrews easily. A valve that drips behind the tile is different. The cartridge sits inside the wall, and getting to it means removing the trim and sometimes opening the tile.

Tub faucets that will not shut off fully waste water all day, and a leak behind a shower wall can soften drywall for weeks before a stain appears. If you hear a trickle after the shower is off, or the wall around the handle feels damp, call sooner rather than later.

Slow shower or tub drain

Standing water at your ankles is often hair and soap in the trap, not a faucet problem. A drain cable clears it in most homes, as described under drain cleaning. If the tub and the toilet back up together, treat it as a deeper line issue.

Local water

Mineral scale and Riverton faucets

Riverton's 2022 water quality report lists average hardness of about 190 mg/L, around 11 grains per gallon. That kind of mineral content leaves chalky scale on aerators and inside cartridges, so faucets lose flow and handles get stiff. Unscrew your aerator, soak it and check for grit. Test your tap if you want a number for your own supply.

Scale on the cartridge is also a good reason to replace a whole cartridge, rather than patching a seal, if the faucet is a few years old.

Outdoor faucets are a separate story. Riverton's irrigation system runs on secondary water, and the city asks residents to shut off their secondary valves by March 15. A hose bib that drips in spring after a cold snap may have a split inside, so check it when you turn things back on.

Repair the fixture or replace it?

Repair when

  • The faucet body is not corroded or pitted
  • A cartridge or seat is available for the model
  • The finish is in good shape
  • Only the stop valve under the sink is leaking

Replace when

  • The body leaks at the base or has cracks
  • Parts are no longer made
  • Scale has fused the stem or handle
  • A new faucet also lets us replace tired supply lines

Utility sinks, jetted tubs and kitchen sprayers each have their own quirks. If yours is unusual, describe it on the call and we will say what to expect.

Rules and sources

Rules and sources behind this page

Fixture repairs are judged against the plumbing code Utah adopts. Utah Code 15A-2-103 adopts the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as the state plumbing code, effective July 1, 2026, with Utah amendments on top. That is one reason a loose supply line or a missing shutoff gets corrected during a repair visit instead of being left alone.

To check any plumber before they open a wall, use the Utah Division of Professional Licensing license lookup. Search by name or license number and set the profession to plumber.
