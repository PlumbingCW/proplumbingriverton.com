Repiping in Riverton, UT

Repiping means replacing a home's worn water supply pipes with new material, either the whole house or the failing sections. In Riverton, UT, the decision usually starts with a pattern: another pinhole, rusty water, or pressure that keeps getting worse.

We inspect before we recommend anything, because many homes need a targeted fix instead of new pipe throughout. You get a clear price before work starts.

The big question

Repair a section or repipe the house?

Most homeowners call about one symptom and want to know whether a patch will hold. The honest answer depends on how much pipe is failing and how much of the house it serves. This table is the framework we use on a first inspection.

Spot repair or repipe: a decision guide
What we findLeans towardWhy
One leak in otherwise clean, flexible pipeSpot repairThe pipe is sound, so replacing only the failed piece is enough
Second or third pinhole in the same copper runReplace that runA pattern means the wall of the pipe is thinning along its length
Galvanized lines with rusty water and weak flowRepipeScale builds inside and narrows the opening, so patching rarely restores flow
Old polybutylene-era plastic with failed fittingsRepipeThe material and connections are the concern, not one leak
Leaks in several unrelated roomsRepipeWide failure means more repairs ahead, each one opening a wall

If your home still has galvanized pipe or old polybutylene-era piping, you may be a candidate, and we confirm by inspection rather than from the age of the house. We do not state a home's piping from its neighborhood.

Warning signs

Signs your supply pipes are wearing out

Brown or orange water

Rust-colored flow after the tap has sat unused points to corroding steel.

Pressure that fades

Flow drops when two fixtures run at once, or an entire bathroom runs weak.

Repeat pinholes

Small spray leaks that reappear in different spots along copper pipe.

Stains and damp patches

Spots on walls or ceilings with no roof or fixture explanation.

Metallic taste or smell

Taste changes can come from the pipe, though the water supply can also be the cause.

Before recommending a repipe, we rule out cheaper causes. A single clogged aerator, a partly closed valve or a leak along the buried line can mimic worn supply piping. Our leak detection service separates a failing line from a hidden leak, and a hidden leak may need only a targeted repair.

Local water

Riverton water and the pipes inside your walls

Riverton's 2022 water quality report lists average hardness of about 190 mg/L, around 11 grains per gallon. Water with more dissolved minerals tends to leave scale in fittings and water heaters, so ask the Riverton water division for its latest annual report and test your own tap.

Scale is not the same as pipe corrosion, and we do not blame the water for every leak. When we cut out a section, we look at the inside wall. That shows if the cause is scale, rust or just a bad joint.

The city owns the water line through the meter, and you own the pipe from there to the home. A repipe covers your side, from the house shutoff inward. If the buried run from the meter is also failing, water line repair is a separate scope we price clearly.

Materials

PEX or copper: how the choice works

PEX tubing

  • Flexible, so it snakes through walls with fewer openings
  • Fewer fittings means fewer places to leak
  • Quieter against framing when properly supported
  • Needs protection from sunlight and stays out of certain spaces

Copper

  • A long track record in homes
  • Rigid and strong, with soldered or pressed joints
  • More labor to install and often more wall access
  • Hard water and scale can still affect it over time

Both can last decades when installed well. We explain which material fits your walls, your budget and your tolerance for drywall repair. A mixed approach is common, such as copper stubs at fixtures and PEX through the framing.

How it goes

What happens during a repipe

1

Inspection and plan

We trace the existing lines, test pressure and flow, and decide where new pipe will run.

2

Permit check

Riverton says a building permit is required for work on plumbing systems regulated by the code. Ask the Riverton building department what your job needs, and we handle what is needed.

3

Water off, access made

Water is shut off for the house. We cut small openings in drywall at strategic spots instead of opening whole rooms.

4

Run and connect new lines

New supply lines go in to each fixture, tied to the water heater and shutoffs.

5

Pressure test and restore flow

We pressure test before closing walls, then turn water back on and check every fixture.

6

Patch and cleanup

We patch the access holes to a paint-ready state. Painting and texture matching are separate.

How many days it takes depends on the size of the home and how much of the piping we replace. Water is off for part of that time, so plan for showers, laundry and cooking. We give a schedule once we have seen the house and do not promise a fixed number of days up front.

Rules and sources

Rules and sources behind this page

Whole-home piping replacement is governed by the plumbing code Utah adopts. Utah Code 15A-2-103 adopts the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as the state plumbing code, effective July 1, 2026, with Utah amendments on top. Riverton's building office says a permit is required for work on plumbing systems the code regulates, which is the office to ask about a repipe.

Verify the plumber's class, Journeyman, Residential Journeyman, Master or Residential Master, through the state's license lookup before you sign anything.
