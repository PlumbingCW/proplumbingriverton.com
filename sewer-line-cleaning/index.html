Sewage is backing up: what to do first

  • ✓Stop all water use.No flushing, laundry, dishwasher or showers. Anything you send down the drain comes back up the lowest opening.
  • ✓Keep people and pets out.Raw sewage carries bacteria. Children, pets and anyone with a weakened immune system should stay clear.
  • ✓Do not try to wash it away.Hosing a floor drain or running a tub sends more water into a full pipe.
  • ✓Turn off power to flooded areas only if you can do it dry.If outlets or appliances are wet, leave them alone and tell us when you call.
  • ✓Call the right number.If sewage is coming out of a street manhole, that's the district's line. JBID lists (801) 571-1166 for manholes and piping. For anything life-threatening, call 911.

For a backup inside your home, call our line. We offer same-day service when available, including after hours, and we'll tell you what to expect before we arrive.

Who owns it

Who handles the blockage: the district or you?

Jordan Basin Improvement District

  • Maintains the sewer main under the street.
  • Serves Riverton, South Jordan, Herriman, Bluffdale and Draper, among others.
  • Takes calls about manholes, mains and lift stations at (801) 571-1166.
  • Says it is responsible for its main line, not your service line.

You, the property owner

  • Own the private line from the house to the main.
  • Pay to clear or repair that line.
  • Usually pay for backup damage unless the district knew of a system problem and did not fix it in reasonable time.
  • Should check homeowners coverage, since standard policies often exclude sewer backup.

JBID, formerly South Valley Sewer District, renamed itself in June 2024. Some city pages still use the old name, but it's the same district.

Test it

The cleanout test that tells you where the clog is

1

Find the cleanout

A capped pipe, often in the yard near the house or in a basement or garage. Clear any items stacked around it.

2

Open the cap slowly

Stand back and use gloves. If the line is full, water or sewage can push out.

3

Look inside

Standing water means the blockage likely sits between your house and the main. JBID says to call the district in that case.

4

No water at the cleanout

That points to a problem inside the house, and we can usually clear it from the cleanout or a fixture.

Don't be tempted to open a cleanout on a backed-up line without a bucket and a plan. Most homeowners are happier letting a plumber handle that step.

Causes

What usually blocks a sewer line

Roots

Fine roots enter at joints and cracks, then catch paper and waste. JBID notes that clay pipes from before the 1980s are especially prone.

Grease

JBID says cooking grease does not belong in drains, even with a disposal. It cools and hardens in the lateral.

Flushed items

JBID asks residents not to flush diapers or other disposable hygiene products. They collect in bends and at joints.

A sagging section

A belly in the pipe holds water and solids, so clogs form at the same spot.

Pipe damage

A cracked or collapsed section catches debris no matter how often it's cleared. That's a repair, not a cleaning.

The work

How we clear and confirm a main line

We start with the cleanout when there is one. A heavy-duty cable machine breaks the blockage, and a cutting head deals with roots. If the line is scaled or greasy, hydro jetting follows the cable.

Then we camera the line to confirm the result and look for the reason it clogged. If the video shows a break, a belly or heavy root entry, we'll show you the footage and talk about sewer line repair. We don't guess at a buried problem, we look at it.

Planned work

Grease traps and storm drains

Grease trap and storm drain cleaning are planned jobs, usually for commercial properties. A restaurant trap needs cleaning on a schedule, and a clogged parking-lot storm drain can flood an entrance after heavy rain.

Call with the type of property and what is installed. We'll tell you honestly whether we can do the work or whether it needs a specialty hauler, and our commercial plumbing page covers the rest.
