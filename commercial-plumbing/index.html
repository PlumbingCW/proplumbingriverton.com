Commercial plumbing emergency: what to do right now

A commercial emergency has more moving parts than a house. Several people may be in the building, and the water shutoff may be in a locked room. A few steps in the right order save the most damage.

1

Clear the area

Move people out of wet zones. Water on a tile floor is a slip hazard, and water near outlets is an electrical one.

2

Shut the water

Close the building main, or the tenant space valve if your lease shows one. Staff should know where both are.

3

Kill power near water

Turn off breakers for wet areas only if you can reach the panel without stepping through water.

4

Protect inventory

Lift boxes, paper goods and electronics off the floor and cover shelving with plastic.

5

Call the 24/7 line

Tell us the type of building, what is leaking and whether sewage is involved. For sewage, close the restroom and keep everyone out.

Locating a leak in a commercial building

Commercial leak detection is the call we hear about most often. The honest answer is that it depends on the building. A strip-center unit with a slab floor and a single supply line is a smaller puzzle than a multi-tenant building with shared risers and drop ceilings.

We start with a water meter test, then listen for the leak with acoustic equipment, trace the line with a locator and use a thermal camera when warm lines are involved. Some leaks sit behind walls we cannot open without the owner's say-so. We will explain what is practical before we start, and we will not guess where a buried leak is. We locate it.

Permits

Riverton says a building permit is required for work on plumbing systems regulated by the code. Ask the Riverton building department what your project needs.

Spills are different. For commercial or industrial spills, Jordan Basin Improvement District lists a dedicated line for sewer system spills. If a restaurant drain overflows into a parking lot, call the district as well as us.

Grease traps, storm drains and backflow testing

Food service businesses live and die by their grease lines. A trap that is not cleaned on a schedule will overflow, usually on a busy night. Planned cleaning is far cheaper than a weekend callout, and it is not usually an emergency until something has already gone wrong.

Storm drains in parking lots work the same way: debris builds up quietly, then rain shows the problem. We clean and inspect them as scheduled work.

Backflow assemblies protect the public water supply. Riverton's backflow program requires assemblies to be tested when installed or repaired and then yearly by a certified tester paid by the owner, with reports sent to the city. We repair assemblies that fail, and we can point you to a tester. Related drain work is covered under hydro jetting.

Rules and sources

Rules and sources behind this page

Commercial work follows the same state plumbing code as homes. Utah Code 15A-2-103 adopts the 2024 International Plumbing Code (IPC) as the state plumbing code, effective July 1, 2026, with Utah amendments on top. Ask the property owner or manager which permits apply before work starts, since Riverton's building office handles permits for plumbing systems regulated by the code.

For sewer spills or grease problems tied to a commercial drain, Jordan Basin Improvement District lists a commercial and industrial spill line. According to the district, the main is theirs and the private line is the property owner's.
